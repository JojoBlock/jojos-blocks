Choosing games for your child

Screen-free or browser games — how to choose

Start with your child's age and how much energy they have right now, not with the shiniest option. Screen-free games — coloring, paper puzzles, board games, building and outdoor play — need no device, work anywhere, and are easy to stop and start again. Browser games are best in short, shared sessions: sit beside your child for the first round, talk about what is happening on screen, and agree before you start how long you will play. If a game needs a grown-up to explain it, play that first round together. And if your child would rather draw, build or run around, that is a good choice too — the best game is the one they actually enjoy.

What each type of game teaches

Different games practise different skills. Puzzles and mazes build problem-solving and patience: a child has to try, get stuck, and try another way. Memory and matching games build concentration and recall. Drawing and creativity games build imagination and fine motor control — the same careful hand movements used for writing. Learning games teach letters, numbers, animals and words through repetition, which is how young children remember things. No single type of game does all of this, so a mix over the week is better than one favourite played all day. Watch what your child comes back to: that is usually where they are learning most.
