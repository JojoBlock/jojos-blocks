A short buying guide

Crayons: what to look for

For ages 2 to 4, choose chunky crayons with a thick tip: they are easier to grip, harder to snap, and forgiving of a fist grip. Triangular crayons help little fingers find the tripod grip without being taught. Washable crayons save furniture, clothes and walls, and non-toxic labelling matters when everything goes in the mouth. For ages 5 to 7, slimmer crayons and coloured pencils allow finer detail and staying inside smaller shapes. A small set of good crayons beats a huge set of waxy, pale ones — look for colours that come out strong on the page, not faint.

Sketchbooks and paper

Thick paper is worth paying for: thin paper tears, wrinkles with felt tips and lets marker bleed through to the next page. A spiral-bound sketchbook lies flat, which is much easier for a small child than a book that keeps closing. For colouring pages, ordinary home printer paper is fine. For painting or heavy markers, look for paper made for mixed media. Size matters too: a big page suits a two-year-old's sweeping arm movements, while an older child may prefer a smaller book they can carry and finish.

Ages 2–7: matching supplies to small hands

Ages 2 to 3 need the simplest tools: big crayons, big paper, and no rules. Ages 4 to 5 can handle more colours, first pencils, blunt scissors and glue sticks, and pages with a little more detail. Ages 6 to 7 are ready for finer tips, sketchbooks, watercolours and craft kits with several steps. In every age group, keep the setup small: a few good tools in a box your child can reach beats a cupboard full of supplies they never touch. Replace what runs out, retire what frustrates them, and let them choose the next colour.
