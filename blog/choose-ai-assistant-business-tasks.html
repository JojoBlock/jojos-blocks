Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini or Grok? How to Choose an AI Assistant for Everyday Business Tasks

AI Tools & Online Business

By JoJo's Blocks · 2026-11-18 · 10 min read · ai-assistants, business

If you run a small business or a one-person online shop, you have heard four recommendations: Claude for writing, ChatGPT for everything, Gemini because you already use Google, and Grok because it follows X. All four can be useful; none is right for every task.

Most comparisons try to crown one winner. This one treats the choice like hiring: you would not hire four assistants and pay them all — you would list the jobs, interview two candidates on real work, and keep the one that makes your week shorter. This guide compares the four task by task, gives you a one-week test to run before you pay, and covers Meta Muse, a newer agent-style option. Most small businesses do well with one main assistant, plus a second one for a specific job.

Writing and editing: the interview test

Claude is a sensible first test for long writing: drafting and revising longer documents, keeping tone consistent, and working through a document section by section. Its Projects feature keeps the files for one piece of work in one place. The free plan includes standard chat models with limits; the more capable model and coding help are paid. ChatGPT is the flexible all-rounder, often easier when one task turns into several — a product description becomes a social post, then a short email. Gemini is the natural choice if the document already lives in Google Docs, because you can draft, shorten, or summarize without copying text between apps. Grok can draft and rewrite, but writing is not its strongest reason to choose it. For the long-document work specifically, see Using Claude for Writing and Editing Long Documents.

Test them the same way: take one real document and ask each one to tighten it to half its length, then rewrite it for a first-time customer. Keep the version that needs the least fixing. That is the whole interview — the assistant that leaves you the least cleanup wins.

Email, planning, and everyday admin

The deciding factor is usually where your work already lives. If your business runs on Gmail, Gemini has a clear advantage: on the relevant Google Workspace plans it is built into Gmail, Docs, Sheets, and Drive, so you can draft a reply or summarize a long thread without opening another app. ChatGPT suits planning outside one ecosystem: turning a rough idea into a weekly plan, drafting a reply to a difficult customer, or organizing a launch checklist. Claude suits admin involving long material, such as summarizing a policy document or turning meeting notes into next steps. If your business runs on Google Workspace, see Gemini in Google Workspace.

Use a simple rule before you automate anything: if a task takes you five minutes and the assistant takes four minutes plus two minutes of checking, it has not saved you time. Use AI for the admin you repeat, not the admin you do once.

Research and checking what is happening now

Grok's clearest strength is freshness. It can search live public posts on X and the web, which helps with questions such as "What are people saying about this launch today?" Treat its answers as a starting point: X posts are opinions, rumors, and marketing as often as facts, so verify anything important against a primary source before you repeat it. Gemini is a good choice for research inside Google's world, and ChatGPT also offers web search and deeper research on paid plans. Claude's research feature, on paid plans, suits long documents rather than breaking news. Whatever tool you use, ask for sources, open at least one, and never publish a price, date, statistic, or claim you have only seen in an AI answer.

Spreadsheets and numbers

Gemini is the easiest starting point in Google Sheets: ask it to write a formula from a plain-English description or summarize patterns in a table, all inside the sheet. ChatGPT is useful for work that starts outside Sheets, such as analyzing a downloaded CSV file — upload a small sample first and check it on rows you can verify by hand. Never trust an AI-generated total or formula without checking it, and keep a copy of the original data first. A wrong total that reaches a customer or a tax return is not a small mistake.

Coding help, images, and video

AI coding help suits small, well-defined jobs such as fixing a broken layout or explaining an error message. It is not a substitute for a developer on anything that handles payments, customer data, or security. Coding help is a core paid feature of both ChatGPT and Claude, often chosen for longer work in Claude's case.

For images, ChatGPT and Gemini both include image generation, useful for mockups, social graphics, and blog illustrations. Gemini's higher plans also include video tools, though availability varies. Grok offers image generation on its free plan with limits, but video starts on a paid tier. Claude is primarily a text and coding assistant, so pair it with a separate image tool if images are central to your work. Check the license for any image you publish.

Meta Muse: a fifth option

Meta Muse, launched in the United States on 8 September 2026, is a personal AI agent that can carry out tasks — bookings, shopping, forms, email, and follow-ups — rather than only answering questions. It reached Canada on 18 September 2026, then Japan on 23 October and Singapore on 27 October; its small-business skills are still described as US and Canada only, and Meta has given no date for other countries. It is available to adults 18 and over, and even the free tier asks for a payment card before the agent will act, according to reporting at launch. Pricing reported in November 2026: free with a usage limit (Meta does not publish the free allowance), Power $20/month with 500 million Muse tokens a week, and Maximum $100/month with 3 billion a week. Think of Muse as an errands tool, not a writing or research assistant.

Privacy and data: read this before you paste anything

Check this before you paste business information into any assistant. On ChatGPT personal plans, OpenAI says it may use your content for training unless you opt out in Data Controls or use a temporary chat; Business and Enterprise workspace data is not used for training by default. On Claude commercial plans, Anthropic says content is not used for training by default; on personal plans, a setting controls it. Google says Workspace business data is not used to train models; the same protection does not automatically apply to a personal account. X says it may share public X data and Grok interactions with xAI for training; you can opt out in X settings, going forward only. Muse training on queries is reported as opt-out by default. Never paste passwords, bank details, full customer lists, or medical or legal documents into a personal AI account — and remember that deleting a chat does not undo training that may already have happened.

How to choose in one week

1. List your five most repeated tasks — replying to customer emails, writing a weekly social post, updating a price list, planning next month's content. 2. Pick two assistants to test. Google Workspace users: Gemini and one other. Mainly writing: Claude and ChatGPT. Following trends on X: Grok and one other. 3. Give both the same real task — a real email, document, or product, with the same instructions, on the same day. 4. Score each result. Did it understand the task? How much did you have to fix? Did it save time after checking? Write the scores down — memory flatters the tool you used last. 5. Test the boring parts: finding an old conversation, uploading a file, exporting the result. You will do these a hundred times; the flashy demo you will do once. 6. Check the privacy settings before you pay. 7. Choose one main tool and one backup, and revisit the choice in three months.

Conclusion

The answer is not the best assistant in general, but the best one for the five tasks you repeat most — and to accept that the answer may be two tools, not one. Start free, test on real work for a week, and check the privacy settings. Pay for one plan when a limit keeps stopping you, and cancel anything you do not use. If an AI assistant is making your day longer instead of shorter, the fit is wrong. Hire for the job, not the brand.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to pay for an AI assistant?⌄

No. All four offer a free plan. Pay only when you regularly hit a free-plan limit or need a paid-only feature.

Which is best for writing?⌄

Claude is a good first test for long documents, ChatGPT is a good all-rounder, and Gemini is easiest if the document already lives in Google Docs. Test all three on the same real document and keep the one that needs the least fixing.

Is it safe to paste customer information into these tools?⌄

Not on a personal account without checking the settings first. Personal plans may use your content for training unless you opt out, while business plans generally do not train on workspace data by default. Never paste passwords, bank details, or sensitive customer data into a personal account.

Is Meta Muse available where I live?⌄

As of November 2026, in the US, Canada, Japan, and Singapore, for adults 18 and over. Its small-business skills are US and Canada only.
