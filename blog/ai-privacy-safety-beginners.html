AI Privacy and Safety for Beginners: What Not to Share, and How to Stay in Control

AI Tools & Online Business

By JoJo's Blocks · 2026-11-18 · 10 min read · ai-privacy, safety

An AI assistant feels like a private conversation. It is not. When you type something into ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini, your words travel to a company's servers, sit there for days or months, and may be used to train future versions of the model or read by a human reviewer.

Treat them like a helpful stranger in a café: fine for a draft, a summary, or an idea, and the wrong place for your passwords, your bank details, or your clients' private information.

One note before we start: this is general information about how these tools work, not legal advice. If you handle other people's personal data for work, check the rules that apply to your business or profession before putting any of it into an AI tool.

What actually happens to what you type

Three things can happen to a chat, and they are worth keeping separate.

It is stored. Your chats live on the company's servers, usually for days or months. Deleting a chat removes it from your history, but the company may keep a copy for up to 30 days.

It may be used to train the model. On a personal account, the three big assistants can use your conversations to improve their models unless you switch the setting off. Turning it off only affects future chats — it does not undo training that already happened.

A human may read some of it. All three services say a small subset of chats is reviewed by human reviewers to judge whether answers were accurate or harmful. Google says reviewed Gemini chats can be kept for up to three years, disconnected from your account, even after you delete them.

None of this makes these tools unusable. It makes them tools with a footprint, and the rest of this guide is about shrinking it.

Step 1: Know what never to share

Never paste these into an AI chat:

  • Passwords, PINs, and security codes. Not yours, not anyone else's. An assistant never needs them.
  • Bank, card, and payment details. Account numbers, card numbers, sort codes.
  • Government ID numbers. Social Security, passport, driver's license, and tax ID numbers.
  • Health information. Diagnoses, prescriptions, test results, insurance details — yours or anyone else's.
  • Someone else's private information. A customer's name and address, an employee's salary, a client's contract. You cannot consent on their behalf.
  • Children's details. Names, schools, photos, medical or school records.
  • Work secrets. Trade secrets, unreleased prices, login credentials, anything under a non-disclosure agreement.

The test is simple: if you would not say it to a stranger, do not type it into an AI chat. If a task needs private details, do the maths yourself.

Step 2: Turn off training — the one setting that matters most

On a personal account, all three assistants can use your chats to train their models until you switch the setting off. This is the single most useful privacy action in this guide.

ChatGPT — "Improve the model for everyone." Web: account menu → Settings → Data controls → turn it off. Phone app: sidebar → profile icon → Data controls. On personal plans (Free, Go, Plus, Pro) it is on by default. Thumbs-up and thumbs-down feedback may still be used for training even after you opt out, and voice mode has a separate training control.

Claude — "Help Improve our AI models." Settings → Privacy. Since August 2025, Free, Pro, Max, and Ultra chats can be used for model improvement unless you opt out. With the setting on, Anthropic may keep your data in de-identified form for up to five years; with it off, retention drops to 30 days.

Gemini — Keep Activity. On a personal Google account it is on by default for adults, and while it is on Google says your chats can be used to train its models and a subset can be read by human reviewers. Turn it off at gemini.google.com → Settings & help → Activity → Turn off. With it off, future chats will not be used for training unless you send Google feedback.

Do this on every account you use, on every device — a setting changed on your laptop does not always change the same setting in the phone app.

Step 3: Use the right account for work

A personal account and a work account are not the same product, and the difference matters more than any setting in Step 2.

On Google Workspace, your prompts are customer data, and Google says Workspace does not use customer data for training models without the customer's prior permission. Gemini chats under a Workspace account are not reviewed by human reviewers outside your domain without permission, and an admin can set retention (3, 18, or 36 months) and hold or export chats in Vault.

The same split exists elsewhere: ChatGPT Business, Enterprise, and Edu workspaces are not used for training by default, and neither are Claude Team and Enterprise or the Claude API. If customer, patient, applicant, or supplier data is involved, do the work in the work account — or do not put it into an AI tool at all.

Paying does not buy privacy on a personal account. A paid ChatGPT or Gemini personal plan has the same training and review rules as the free one. The plan that changes the rules is a business or workspace plan, where training is off by default.

Step 4: Use the private modes for one-off questions

Each tool has a mode that keeps a chat out of your history and out of training.

ChatGPT Temporary Chat. It does not appear in your history and is not used to improve the models. OpenAI says it may be kept for up to 30 days for safety.

Claude incognito chats. Anthropic says they are never used to improve its models and do not appear in your chat history. They are still kept for 30 days for safety.

Gemini temporary chats. Google says they do not appear in your activity or recent chats, are kept for 72 hours, and are not used for training.

All three still send your words to the company's servers. A private mode is a smaller footprint, not no footprint — the list in Step 1 still applies.

Step 5: Check what the assistant remembers about you

Modern assistants remember things between chats — your name, your business, your preferences — so they can be more useful. That memory is also a file about you sitting on someone else's server.

In ChatGPT, Memory is viewable, editable, and deletable from Settings. In Claude, memory works through Projects and the files you give it. In Gemini, the assistant draws on your Google account activity and connected apps.

Review memory once a month and delete anything you would not want a stranger to know — the assistant does not need your home address, your children's names, or your bank. And never let memory do the work of a password manager: if the assistant knows it, it is stored somewhere you do not control.

Step 6: Spot the scams that use AI against you

AI has made the old scams harder to spot, because it removes the mistakes that used to give them away. The US Federal Trade Commission reported consumers lost more than $12.5 billion to fraud in 2024, up 25% on the year before. That figure covers all fraud, not just AI-assisted fraud — the FTC does not break out how much involved AI, so nobody can honestly give you an AI-only number.

Three scams to know by name:

The voice-clone family emergency. A call from a voice that sounds exactly like your child or grandchild: an accident, an arrest, money needed now. The FTC warns a scammer needs only a short audio clip of a person's voice — easily taken from content posted online — and a voice-cloning program. The ask always comes with urgency and secrecy.

The deepfake video call. The FBI warns criminals use AI-generated video in real-time chats, posing as executives, officials, or loved ones, and AI images of celebrities to promote fake products.

Phishing with perfect grammar. The typo-riddled phishing email is becoming a period piece. The FBI notes generative AI corrects the human errors that used to warn people, and powers fictitious profiles and fraudulent investment sites.

The defense is procedural, not technical:

  1. Agree a family code word. A secret word or phrase only your family knows. If a "relative" in trouble cannot say it, hang up.
  2. Hang up and call back. Never act on an urgent call. Call the person back on a number you already know is theirs.
  3. Never pay the way a scammer asks. Wire transfers, cryptocurrency, and gift cards are hard or impossible to reverse. That is why scammers ask for them.
  4. Report it. In the US, report fraud to the FTC at reportfraud.ftc.gov and to the FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center at ic3.gov. If money moved, contact your bank first.

No tool reliably detects every cloned voice or fake video. The code word and the call-back are the defense.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Assuming a paid plan is private. On a personal account, paying changes the limits, not the data rules.
  • Pasting private details into a chat "just this once." The words are stored the moment you send them.
  • Trusting a private mode completely. Temporary and incognito chats are a smaller footprint, not no footprint.
  • Letting the assistant remember everything. Review memory monthly and delete what it does not need.
  • Believing a familiar voice. A voice clone sounds like your family. A code word and a call-back are the only proof.

Conclusion

AI privacy is about knowing where your words go, and acting on it: turn off training on every account, keep private details out of the chat, use the work account for work, and use a private mode for a one-off sensitive question.

Start today: open each assistant you use, turn off the training setting, and delete the memories it does not need. Then agree a family code word.

Frequently asked questions

Does paying for ChatGPT or Gemini stop them using my chats for training?⌄

No — not on a personal account. The training switch is the same on free and paid personal plans. Business and Workspace plans do not train on your content by default.

If I delete a chat, is it gone?⌄

It disappears from your history straight away, but the company may keep a copy for a short safety window — up to 30 days. A chat picked for human review can be kept longer: Google says reviewed Gemini chats can be kept for up to three years, even after deletion.

How do I protect my family from AI voice-clone scams?⌄

Agree a secret code word with your family, and make a rule: any urgent call asking for money gets hung up on, and you call the person back on a number you already know. Never pay by wire transfer, cryptocurrency, or gift cards.
