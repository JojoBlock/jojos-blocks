What Do Community Helpers Do?

Education

By JoJo's Blocks · 2026-11-15 · 6 min read · community helpers, helpers for kids, jobs for kids

Every day, all around us, people are doing jobs that help our towns work. The firefighter who keeps us safe, the teacher who helps us learn, the farmer who grows our food — they are all community helpers. Here are ten helpers to meet with your child, what each one does, the tools they use, and a simple way to play and learn about them together.

1. Community helpers for preschool — what is a community helper?

A community helper is a person whose job is to help the people who live in their town or neighborhood. Helpers keep us safe, keep us healthy, teach us new things, bring us what we need, and fix things when they break. Your child already meets helpers every day — at school, at the library, and at the doctor's office. Talking about what each helper does is a simple way to help your child notice the people around them and say thank you.

2. Helpers who keep us safe

Firefighter

Firefighters put out fires and help people in emergencies. They drive fire trucks, connect hoses to hydrants, climb ladders, and rescue people from burning buildings. They also help sick or injured people and practice drills, so they are ready when an alarm sounds. Their tools include a hose, a helmet, a ladder, and a fire truck. Play idea: set up a pretend fire station with a cardboard-box truck, and spray water on a chalk "fire" outside, with an adult nearby.

Police officer

Police officers protect lives and property. They patrol neighborhoods, answer calls for help, direct traffic, and help people who are lost or in trouble. Their tools include a badge, a radio, a notebook, and a patrol car. Play idea: play traffic lights in the yard — green means go, yellow means slow down, red means stop — and take turns being the officer who directs the traffic.

3. Helpers who keep us healthy

Doctor

Doctors find out what is wrong when we feel sick or hurt, and help us get better. They listen to our hearts with a stethoscope, look in our ears and throats, and give us medicine or a bandage when we need one. Their tools include a stethoscope, a thermometer, and a bandage. Play idea: open a toy doctor's office and give a stuffed animal a checkup — listen, look, and put on a bandage.

Nurse

Nurses care for people who are sick or hurt, in hospitals, clinics, and schools. They check temperatures, give medicine, change bandages, and explain what will happen next. Their tools include a thermometer, a bandage, and a blood pressure cuff. Play idea: let your child be the nurse for a doll, taking its temperature and tucking it into bed.

Veterinarian

A veterinarian is an animal doctor. Vets check pets, give them shots to keep them healthy, and help animals that are hurt or sick. Their tools include a stethoscope, a thermometer, and medicine, just like a doctor for people. Play idea: set up a vet clinic for stuffed animals, with a waiting room, a checkup table, and a bandage for every patient.

4. Helpers who help us learn

Teacher

Teachers help children learn to read, write, count, and explore new ideas. They plan lessons, read stories aloud, answer questions, and help children practice new skills. Their tools include books, pencils, paper, and a whiteboard or chalkboard. Play idea: play school — your child can be the teacher, read a picture book to their toys, and write the day's lesson on a piece of paper.

Librarian

Librarians help people find books and information. They put books on shelves, help you choose what to read next, read stories aloud, and keep the library quiet for learning. Their tools include books, shelves, and a library card. Play idea: make a small library at home — arrange books on a shelf, make library cards, and let your child "check out" a book to read.

5. Helpers who keep our town running

Postal worker

Postal workers collect, sort, and deliver the mail. They bring letters, cards, and packages to homes and businesses, on foot or in a delivery truck. Their tools include a mailbag, letters and packages, and a delivery truck. Play idea: write a letter or draw a picture, put it in an envelope, and let your child deliver it to a neighbor or a family member.

Farmer

Farmers grow the food we eat. They plant seeds, water crops, care for animals, and harvest fruits and vegetables when they are ripe. Their tools include a tractor, seeds, a shovel, and a watering can. Play idea: plant a seed in a cup, water it together, and watch it grow — or set up a pretend farm stand and "sell" play food.

Builder

Builders make the places we live, learn, and play in — houses, schools, and libraries. They measure, cut, hammer, and paint, working from plans to turn a drawing into a real building. Their tools include a hard hat, a hammer, a measuring tape, and a saw. Play idea: build a tower or a house from blocks, measure it with a tape, and draw a plan before you start.

6. Play the matching game

Matching each helper to their tools is a fun way to remember what each job is. Lay out pictures of helpers on one side and pictures of their tools on the other, and ask your child to draw a line from each helper to the tools they use. Draw the cards together, or use the matching activity in My Little Community Helpers. Here are the pairs to start with:

  • Firefighter — hose, helmet, and fire truck.
  • Police officer — badge, radio, and patrol car.
  • Doctor — stethoscope, thermometer, and bandage.
  • Nurse — thermometer, bandage, and blood pressure cuff.
  • Veterinarian — stethoscope, thermometer, and medicine.
  • Teacher — books, pencils, and a whiteboard.
  • Librarian — books, shelves, and a library card.
  • Postal worker — mailbag, letters, and a delivery truck.
  • Farmer — tractor, seeds, and a watering can.
  • Builder — hard hat, hammer, and measuring tape.

Start with two or three helpers, then add more as your child gets the hang of it. If a match is tricky, talk about it together and try again.

7. Easy ways to explore helpers together — activities and coloring pages

  • Point out helpers on walks — the crossing guard, the bus driver, the shopkeeper — and name what each one does.
  • Say thank you together. A wave, a smile, or a thank-you card can mean a lot to a helper.
  • Read and color about helpers. My Little Community Helpers has 24 helpers to meet and color, with simple job descriptions and a matching activity.
  • Try a free sample first — the Free Taster Pack includes a firefighter coloring page to try at home.
  • Keep it short and fun — about 10 to 20 minutes of coloring or play is plenty, and let your child stop whenever they lose interest.

Frequently asked questions

What age is this article for?⌄

It is written for parents to share with children ages 4 to 7, the same age range as My Little Community Helpers. Younger children can enjoy the pictures, the helper names, and the matching game too.

How can I teach my child about community helpers?⌄

Point helpers out on walks and errands, talk about what each one does, and let your child play the job — a toy doctor's kit, a pretend post office, or a block-building site. Coloring a helper page and talking about it can be just as valuable as a longer lesson.

How does the matching game work?⌄

Lay out pictures of helpers on one side and pictures of tools on the other, and ask your child to match each helper to the tools they use. Start with two or three helpers and add more over time. The matching activity in My Little Community Helpers works the same way.
