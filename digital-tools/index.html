How to choose a digital tool

AI tools

AI tools can help with writing, art, video and everyday planning, but they vary a lot. Before paying, try the free plan and check three things: what it actually costs after any trial ends, whether your work stays private, and whether you can export or download what you make. A tool that locks your work inside it is a bad deal, however clever it is. Keep children's names, photos and school details out of AI tools, and treat anything an AI writes or draws as a first draft to check, not a finished result.

Image and video tools

Image tools turn words into pictures; video tools turn clips into finished videos. For a beginner, the best tool is the one with the shortest path from idea to result — templates, simple controls and honest pricing matter more than a long feature list. Check what the free version lets you export: watermarked or low-resolution exports are fine for trying things out, but not for work you want to share or sell. Always check the licence on images you make, especially if they will appear on a website or product.

Design and productivity tools

Design software is for posters, social posts, printables and presentations; productivity tools are for planning, notes and staying organised. Pick one tool and learn it properly rather than collecting five you barely use. Free plans are usually enough for family and hobby use — pay only when a limit you actually hit, like storage or exports, is getting in your way. And back up your work somewhere you control: a tool's cloud is convenient, but your files should also live on your own device.

Learning platforms and courses

A good learning platform teaches one skill at a time, in short lessons, with something to make or try at the end of each one. For children, look for platforms with no advertising, no chat with strangers, and clear age guidance. For adults, check whether the course is taught by someone who actually does the skill, and whether there are real student results — not just a sales page. Free introductory lessons are the honest way to judge a course before paying.
